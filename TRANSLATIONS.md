# Translating Vastrik: same person, another language

## The job

Translate the Russian post as closely as natural target-language writing allows. The reader should feel that **Vastrik is sitting next to them in a bar, explaining something interesting, sharing what he learned, admitting where he fucked up, and having a good time telling the story.**

The purpose is to pass on understanding, experience and emotion. The reader should understand a difficult thing, recognize something from their own life, or feel curious enough to try something. Keep the person doing the explaining: his enthusiasm, irritation, affection, doubts, jokes, bad decisions and hard-earned experience.

This is a translation brief, not a license to write a new post “in the style of Vastrik.” Preserve the actual argument and story. Do not invent anecdotes, opinions, expertise, jokes or dramatic failures to make the result sound more like a blogger.

**Profanity is explicitly welcome.** Fuck, shit, bullshit, fucking hell, and idiomatic stronger language are valid translation tools. Do not censor, asterisk, apologize for, or automatically soften Russian obscenity. There is no advertiser-friendly requirement and no fixed swear-word budget. Translate emotional force and intent, not just dictionary meaning. Equally, a quiet paragraph must remain quiet: indiscriminately adding swearing would be another kind of mistranslation.

These priorities come directly from the author's brief on 2026-11-18. They take precedence over the weaker or freer choices found in some existing translations.

## Evidence and limits of this guide

Research snapshot: **2026-11-18**. The [Russian RSS](https://vas3k.blog/rss/) contained **30 items**, dated 2022-06-01 through 2026-05-11. The feed contains previews, so all 30 linked public pages were downloaded, not just their RSS descriptions. Four entries lead to public Club posts. This is the complete feed snapshot, **not the complete historical blog archive** and not a claim that an RSS publication date is the date of the latest edit.

The analysis surveyed the opening, structure, selected body passages, captions and ending of every feed article, with targeted closer comparison of translation choices. Reader comments, navigation and Club author-profile cards were excluded from extracted article text. Text inside images was not comprehensively transcribed; a real translation must separately check those assets. Some Club posts credit multiple authors, so their shared event-report voice is corroborating evidence, not proof that every sentence is Vasily's alone.

Additional references:

- The author's [А как делать блог?](https://vas3k.blog/notes/how_to_blog/) describes writing for a specific real person, making explanations understandable, reading aloud, short paragraphs and the importance of illustrations. Its bar-conversation description directly supports this brief.
- [Мат в постах](https://vas3k.blog/365/2018-03-23/) treats swearing as an expressive instrument and distinguishes emotional stories from passages whose atmosphere needs restraint. Its historical observations about sharing technical articles are not a present-day requirement to sanitize them.
- The classic [blockchain explainer](https://vas3k.blog/blog/blockchain/) and [machine-learning explainer](https://vas3k.blog/blog/machine_learning/) demonstrate the recognizable progression from everyday situations to technical concepts. Their English versions were also downloaded.
- All published translations linked by the feed articles were downloaded: **nine English articles and one Spanish article**. With the two classics, the comparison corpus contains eleven English versions and one Spanish version. No Chinese or Hindi article pair was examined; guidance for those languages below is a proposed application of the brief, not an established house precedent.
- A [public Club discussion of writing a ChatGPT explainer](https://vas3k.club/link/18671/) explicitly points to Vastrik's blockchain explanation as a model for accessible concepts and analogies. This is evidence of how other writers describe the style, not authority to turn it into a caricature.

The download set contains 46 article pages, their extracted text, two RSS files and manifests. The observations below distinguish **what the sources do** from **what future translations should do**. Existing versions may reflect edits or adaptations at different dates; a difference is not, by itself, proof of who introduced it or why.

## What makes the voice recognizable

### A real experience gives the explanation a reason to exist

A problem comes before its abstract name. In [Vibe Coding](https://vas3k.blog/notes/vibe_coding/), the need to read French restaurant menus leads into building an app. In [TaxHacker](https://vas3k.blog/notes/taxhacker/), receipts and bureaucracy give the software a personal reason to exist. In [the bow article](https://vas3k.blog/notes/bow/), enthusiasm for a gift leads into how the equipment works.

Keep that order. Do not replace the opening scene with a definition, executive summary or “In today's rapidly evolving technological landscape.” Do not promote a personal experiment into a universal recommendation.

### Explanation happens through things the reader can picture

Debts between friends, buying a used car, sending memes, pets and cattle: familiar examples carry the mechanism. The terminology arrives when the reader has something to attach it to. The narrator walks through the model, points out what breaks, and builds the next version.

Preserve the whole analogy and its progression, including callbacks. A technically correct abbreviation of the argument can still ruin the explanation. Track which person has which key, who owes whom money, and which animal represents what. Keep technical qualifications and uncertainty as carefully as the punchlines.

### He talks with the reader, not down from a podium

Direct address, imagined objections, questions, commands, fragments and little conversational turns keep a long post moving. Invitations to try something or share experience often close a post. A short punchline gets its own paragraph because the pause is part of the performance.

Use ordinary spoken syntax and contractions where natural. Preserve purposeful repetition and interruptions. Do not mechanically translate every “ну,” “вот,” “короче” or “как бы”; reproduce their conversational function. Avoid both stiff formal prose and an invented frat-boy persona shouting “bro” in every paragraph.

### Technical competence coexists with self-mockery

The narrator can explain a system and also admit he is learning, guessing, overspending or making a mess. In [the camper update](https://vas3k.blog/blog/bus_2022/) and [home-server retrospective](https://vas3k.blog/notes/homelab_2022/), the unglamorous compromises matter as much as the successful build.

Keep the joke aimed where the source aims it. Do not turn “I did something stupid” into “users are stupid,” or turn affectionate mockery into contempt. Equally, do not soften a deliberate attack on a product or bureaucracy into bland constructive feedback.

### His voice has a wide emotional range

[The 2025 retrospective](https://vas3k.blog/blog/2025/) moves between disappointment, self-doubt, projects and pleasure in people. [The 2022 retrospective](https://vas3k.blog/blog/2022/) opens with grief and loss of hope. [AfrikaBurn](https://vas3k.blog/world/afrikaburn26/) mixes logistics, mistakes, affection and delight.

The bar metaphor means intimacy, not permanent comedy. Preserve sincerity, grief, tenderness and unresolved feelings. Do not add a reassuring moral, upbeat conclusion or joke to make a painful passage easier to market.

### Internet language is texture, not a costume

Deliberate misspellings, diminutives, English borrowings, exaggerated capitalization, emoji, fake corporate voices and affectionate object names are recurrent tools. They mark stance: nerdy familiarity, mock grandeur, embarrassment, exasperation or affection.

Find a target-language device with the same job. Do not translate all diminutives as “little,” all slang as “fuck,” or every intentionally silly word as a spelling error. Do not add fashionable slang just because the original sounds informal.

## Fidelity: what can change and what must not

| Preserve | May adapt to sound natural | Do not silently do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Facts, numbers, dates, names, causes and consequences | Word order, idioms, grammatical structure | Update historical claims or exchange rates |
| First-person experience, limits of knowledge, uncertainty | Sentence boundaries within a paragraph | Invent confidence, experience or supporting evidence |
| Argument order and the steps of an explanation | Local connective phrases and rhythm | Summarize away a difficult section |
| Joke premise, target, escalation and payoff | Pun wording or a familiar idiom with the same function | Replace the author's opinion or add a new joke |
| Emotional strength, including obscenity | Placement and grammatical form of a swear | Make the author more polite or more hostile |
| Real people, places and cultural setting | A brief identifying gloss when needed | Relocate the story or turn German life into American life |
| Links, credits, code, quantities and qualifications | Displayed link text and human-readable labels | Remove attribution or silently alter executable examples |

Default to keeping names such as Oleg. Existing English explainers sometimes use Bill/Billy, and the encryption translation uses Greg. That is a historical adaptation, not blanket permission to rename everyone. If maintaining an existing translated series with established names, keep a consistent character map across prose, diagrams and captions; document the choice. Never rename real friends, authors or collaborators for localization.

Translate directly from the Russian source where possible. An existing English version is a useful reference, not a pivot that overrides the Russian text. Otherwise its omissions and softening will propagate into every other language.

## Humor: preserve the mechanism and the pause

1. **Identify the joke before translating.** Is it a reversal, mock-serious explanation, absurd exaggeration, cultural reference, intentional typo, self-own, quoted voice, or an image/text reveal?
2. **Keep the setup intact.** Do not give away the ending early or move the explanation ahead of the joke.
3. **Keep the target and stance.** Sarcasm about the narrator's own purchase is not a claim that the reader is an idiot. A borrowed corporate phrase may be funny precisely because it is painfully formal.
4. **Try the closest natural rendering first.** If the literal version is funny and intelligible, keep it. Literal grotesque imagery can be deliberate; don't automatically replace every unusual image with a stock English idiom.
5. **Adapt only the part that fails.** Recreate a pun or reference locally while preserving its point. Prefer a small inline cue over a paragraph explaining why something is funny. Put unresolved alternatives in translator notes outside the article.
6. **Read the preceding and following lines aloud.** The translated punchline must arrive at the same beat. Keep short paragraphs, emphatic caps, strikethroughs, spoiler reveals and meaningful emoji.

Examples of source-specific decisions:

- [The toilet story](https://vas3k.blog/notes/toilets/) depends on repeated bureaucratic escalation and visual reveals. Preserve its stated fictional framing and reconstructed-image caveat; do not present the story as a verified report about an identifiable employer. Keep the image order and pauses.
- [The Makita article](https://vas3k.blog/notes/makita/) develops a Hogwarts-house comparison around tool brands. In that context, translating the title's “факультет” as “house” can preserve the joke better than “faculty.” Keep the same metaphor throughout.
- [Flipper Zero](https://vas3k.blog/notes/flipperzero/) includes a device-name rhyme. Retain the real device identifier. If its Russian rhyme cannot survive, a tiny explanation is more faithful than inventing a different identifier.
- A cultural reference may remain unfamiliar without breaking the story. Explain only what the reader needs to follow the joke or the argument. Do not replace every Russian reference with an American celebrity.

## Swearing without sanitizing it

**Treat profanity as part of the meaning.** No euphemism pass, no “professionalizing” pass, no automatic conversion of strong obscenity into “darn,” “heck,” “very,” “unfortunate,” or “challenging.” Do not ask for permission every time the source swears; the author has already given it.

Translate the **function, intensity, direction and mood** of the expression. Russian obscene roots are productive: the same root can describe admiration, exhaustion, deception, indifference or failure. A word-for-word anatomical translation is often much less faithful than an idiomatic fucking swear.

The table below contains **translator-generated options**, not a mandatory substitution dictionary and not quotations from published English versions. Context decides.

| Russian expression | Possible English rendering | What to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| заебись / охуенно | fucking great; fucking awesome | Enthusiasm, unless the context makes it sarcastic |
| Заебись сэкономил! | Great fucking savings! | Bitter self-mockery after spending more, not genuine praise |
| пиздец | a total fucking disaster; we're fucked; holy shit | Disaster, helplessness or amazement: choose the actual sense |
| хуйня | shit; bullshit; this fucking thing | Bad quality, a false claim and an unnamed object are different meanings |
| хуёво | shitty; like shit; fucking badly | A blunt negative judgment |
| охуеть | holy shit; what the fuck; fucking hell | Surprise, disbelief or anger |
| мне похуй / мне насрать | I don't give a fuck / I don't give a shit | Indifference, not agreement |
| нахуя? | why the fuck?; what the fuck for? | A question about purpose |
| блять | fuck; fucking; for fuck's sake | An exclamation or intensifier; usually not a literal personal insult |
| заебался | I'm fucking exhausted; I'm sick of this shit | Fatigue versus fed-up irritation |
| заебёшься искать | you'll have a fucking nightmare finding it | Difficulty/exhaustion, not somebody sexually harming the subject |
| ебашить | blast; hammer away; work your ass off | Determine whether this is sound, effort, action or impact |
| наебать | rip off; bullshit; fuck over | Deception or exploitation |
| доебаться | nitpick; give someone shit; find something to bitch about | The particular kind of pestering or fault-finding |
| обосрались | we fucked up; we screwed the pooch | Failure and embarrassment; keep literal shit only when it belongs to the image |
| всратый | shitty; janky; cursed; gloriously ugly | Sometimes affectionate ugliness, not outright hatred |
| ебеня | the middle of fucking nowhere | Remoteness and comic inconvenience |
| разъеб | fucking incredible; absolutely fucking killed it | In praise, it is not a literal disaster |
| пиздеть | talk shit; bullshit; run your mouth | Chatter, lying and empty boasting need different verbs |
| долбоёб | fucking idiot; dumbass | Who is being insulted, how strongly, and whether it is affectionate/self-directed |

English can redistribute the force. A Russian verb may carry the obscenity inside it; English may need an adverb, idiom or a nearby “fucking.” That is not gratuitous added profanity. But adding a swear to every sentence, or replacing every swear with the strongest available insult, destroys the author's range.

Preserve the difference between swearing **about a situation** and insulting **a person**. Do not accidentally turn an impersonal expletive into a new accusation about somebody's family, sexuality or identity. This is semantic fidelity, not a politeness restriction. Translate an intentional insult as an intentional insult; do not add a new target.

Creative compounds are welcome when they sound native and match the source: “clusterfuck,” “bullshit factory,” or “fuck-all” can work in the right context. They are not interchangeable decorations. A fucking excellent paragraph may need no instance of the word “fuck” if the original gets its energy elsewhere.

## Applying the voice to each language

### English

Use natural conversational English: contractions, short verbs, precise technical terms, fragments where intentional. Keep the author's Russian/German/European life rather than moving him into a generic American tech scene. Use a consistent regional spelling convention; don't switch between conspicuously British and American slang at random.

“Fuck,” “shit,” “bullshit,” “fucking,” “fuck-all,” “clusterfuck” and stronger expressions are available. Pick native constructions, not Russian grammar filled with English swear words. Avoid automatic slang padding such as “dude,” “bro,” “epic” or “insane.” Preserve an affectionate nickname if it is readable; explain it once when genuinely necessary.

### Spanish

Choose the intended regional variety and keep its address forms and slang consistent. Do not arbitrarily mix Spain-specific and Latin American expressions to make the voice seem more obscene. A broad Spanish edition still needs a coherent speaking voice.

Vulgar expressions built around “joder” or “mierda” are legitimate tools, not forbidden words. For example, **“es una puta mierda”** can preserve a strongly dismissive judgment about a thing; it is not a universal equivalent for every Russian swear. Match the emotion, not merely the presence of a dictionary label. [Wiktionary's entry for joder](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/joder) documents its different vulgar senses and regional usage.

The one Spanish article inspected is insufficient to establish a complete Spanish house style. Translate against Russian, then assess whether the result sounds like one person talking naturally, rather than a second translation of awkward English.

### Chinese

Choose the intended locale and script explicitly; the code's `zh` language key alone does not settle the editorial audience. Use spoken, readable Chinese rather than a formal explanatory essay with jokes bolted on.

Use native colloquial and obscene resources without sanitization. For example, **他妈的** can function as a swear/intensifier, while **牛逼** can express vulgar admiration in Mainland Mandarin. They do different jobs; neither is a universal mapping for Russian mat. Consult the entries for [他媽的](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/他媽的) and [牛屄 / 牛逼](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/牛屄), then judge the full sentence and audience. Preserve comic warmth and force rather than stacking literal anatomical curses.

Recreate playful misspellings or diminutives through natural local phrasing, rhythm or wordplay; transliterating Russian silliness character by character usually loses its function. This is proposed guidance: no Chinese parallel article was assessed in this research.

### Hindi

Choose the target audience's natural spoken register, including Hindi-English mixing where it actually belongs. Do not turn an intimate technology conversation into excessively formal, Sanskritized prose. Keep technical vocabulary understandable and consistent; preserve proper names, commands and API identifiers.

Use genuine idiomatic Hindi obscenity when the Russian warrants it. Do not default to mild substitutions, but do not choose a family-directed insult merely because a dictionary labels it strong: that can change who is being attacked. Address forms, grammatical gender and the degree of familiarity also affect the voice.

No Hindi parallel article was assessed here, so this document does not invent an “approved” Hindi swear-word glossary. A fluent target-language pass should judge idiom and force against the Russian brief, not remove the swearing.

For any additional language, apply the same method: choose a coherent locale/register, preserve facts and jokes, use local profanity with equivalent intent, then read it as speech. Regional fluency matters more than a mechanical severity ranking.

## Keep the article's structure and machinery intact

This section complements `AGENTS.md`; it does not authorize publishing or database changes.

- Translate the full supplied source: title, subtitle, body, captions, image labels, alt text, spoilers, footnotes, updates, credits and relevant social-preview text. Identify text embedded in images as separate work. Do not fabricate a translation of an unread screenshot.
- Work from original Markdown/HTML when available. RSS snippets and scraped plain text are research aids: they lose layout, links and reveal timing. A standalone `?` in extracted text can be a spoiler button, not author punctuation.
- Preserve custom blocks `[[[...]]]`, `{{{...}}}`, `[? ... ?]`, citation syntax, classes, explicit IDs and embed URLs. Translate human-facing text inside them, not their delimiters or machine-readable attributes.
- Preserve code, commands, API names, equations, amounts, units, filenames and example relationships. Translate comments or diagram labels where useful without breaking runnable code or consistency.
- Heading text can change generated anchors. Verify internal links and translated diagram references; do not blindly carry over a Russian anchor that the translated heading no longer generates.
- Keep block order and inline-comment/reaction identities when editing an existing article. Do not merge/split custom blocks merely to make the translation look tidy.
- For a new translated Post, keep the intended shared slug and type and set the target language. The UI catalogs in `locale/` translate the interface; they do not contain article translations. Preserve the Russian record and edition-specific comments/counters.
- Prefer a verified translated destination for a link when it exists. Otherwise keep the original destination; do not invent an English URL or remove the link because its target is Russian.

## Translation workflow and acceptance check

1. **Read the complete source before drafting.** Note genre, emotional arc, key analogy, recurring characters, terms, jokes, vulgar expressions, callbacks and image dependencies. Record source URL/version and target locale.
2. **Draft for completeness.** Keep the paragraph/section sequence and all factual content. Mark genuinely ambiguous passages in working notes instead of silently guessing.
3. **Make it sound spoken.** Replace calques with native syntax, restore pauses, and preserve abrupt little punchlines. Do not shorten by deleting personal material.
4. **Audit jokes and obscenity separately.** For each strong phrase, ask what it does and whether the target preserves that force. Catch both censorship and exaggerated aggression. Check sarcasm has not become sincere praise.
5. **Compare against Russian paragraph by paragraph.** Check omissions, additions, negation, numbers, dates, qualifications, references, names and the ending. Existing translations are secondary references only.
6. **Read aloud in the target language.** A competent native speaker should sound like a person telling a story. Repair awkward grammar without sanding off intentional rough edges. If no fluent review was available, state that in the handoff rather than claiming it happened.
7. **Check the rendered article.** Inspect captions, screenshots, spoilers, links, narrow-screen paragraph lengths and visual reveals. Keep translator notes outside the published prose.

A translation is ready when:

- The same things happen, in the same explanatory sequence, to the same people.
- Facts, numbers, code, uncertainty and attribution survive.
- The jokes still work, including the last joke and image-dependent ones.
- Strong language remains strong; warm language remains warm; grief remains grief.
- It reads naturally without becoming more corporate, more academic or more performatively edgy.
- No extra moral, sales pitch, historical update or invented personal experience has appeared.
- Formatting and links still work, and any unresolved choices are named in the handoff.

For future translation tasks, the reusable brief is:

> Translate this Russian Vastrik post into [language and locale], using `TRANSLATIONS.md`. Stay close to the source in meaning, structure, facts, emotion and humor. Sound like the author explaining the subject to a friend in a bar. Preserve self-mockery, personal details, technical clarity, short spoken rhythms and uncensored profanity. Adapt idioms and wordplay only as much as natural target-language speech requires. Do not summarize, sanitize, invent jokes, upgrade facts or add swearing indiscriminately. Preserve markup, code, links, captions and callbacks. Put unresolved translation choices outside the article.
